Every Monday morning, one economics student records what the same fixed basket costs at three Folsom grocery stores — Walmart, Target, and Safeway — item by item, straight from each retailer's own published store prices. National CPI tells you what food costs on average, months later. This tells you what it costs here, this week — measured, not estimated.

01 — The number

Grocery inflation, measured on foot

The index is a weighted average of price changes across five food categories, benchmarked to the first week of the official series (= 100). Weights are fixed, BLS-style food-at-home category weights, published in the page source, so a move in this line means the same thing a CPI move does — just for Folsom, and weekly instead of monthly.

04 — The method

How it's measured

1

Fixed basket

The same named items, in the same package sizes, at the same stores — every single week. No substitutions; if an item is out of stock, it's flagged and its prior-week price is carried forward (standard index imputation), never substituted. The two meat items are specified exactly: store-brand conventional 80/20 ground beef and boneless-skinless chicken breast, priced per pound from the standard tray closest to one pound — never value packs, chubs, or premium lines.

2

Collected consistently

Prices recorded every Monday morning from each retailer's own website or app, with the exact Folsom store location selected. Prices are recorded as displayed for that store, including free loyalty-program pricing where the retailer shows it (Safeway for U); clip-to-activate coupons and paid-membership prices are never used.

3

Weighted like the CPI

Category price relatives are combined with BLS-style food-at-home weights (a Laspeyres index), so the composite behaves like a true price index, not a raw price average.

4

Published open

Every observation lives in a public CSV on GitHub. Anyone can audit the math, rebuild the index, or extend it to their own city.

REVISION LOG
2026-08-21 — The seven collection weeks of Jul 6 – Aug 17 are designated the pilot phase. During the pilot, selection of the two meat items was not governed by a written specification, so those observations are not like-for-like week to week. The index previously displayed on this site, computed from all pilot data with base week Jul 6, is superseded and is not comparable to the official series. Pilot rows remain published in the CSV, flagged pilot, and are excluded from the index.
2026-08-21 — The store panel is set at three stores: Walmart, Target, and Safeway. The pilot panel also included Bel Air, removed to avoid a potential conflict of interest for the author (see the README). Its 98 pilot observations remain published at archive/belair.csv.
2026-08-22 — Produce and packaged-item rules finalized ahead of the base week: bananas are priced per banana at all three stores (Target displays only a per-each price, making per-each the unit observable everywhere), and Safeway’s 22 oz store-brand loaf is normalized to a 20 oz equivalent via its per-ounce price. Pilot rows for Target bananas were corrected from an inferred per-pound figure to the observed per-each price ($0.29, unit per banana).
2026-08-21 — Collection protocol adopted in writing (see the README): exact item specifications for both meat items, and the price-type rule under which Safeway prices are the store’s free-membership (for U) prices — the same instrument every week. The official series begins with the first collection under this protocol, Monday, Aug 24, 2026 = 100.

This is an independent student research project. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by, or informed by any retailer's internal data — every price is the retailer's own publicly listed price for its named Folsom store. Store names can be anonymized on request.
